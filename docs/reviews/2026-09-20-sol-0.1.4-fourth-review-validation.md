**What I changed in how this rule is written, and why it matters more than the four repairs.**
Five reviews, five failures, and the pattern is now plain: the reviews are not finding the same
defect — each finds states the previous text did not list, and twice the repair created the next
two. Git branching × batch states × fixes × gates has more states than a paragraph and a table
will enumerate. A rule that is correct only if it lists every state cannot converge under
adversarial review, and would not be obeyable if it did. So the rule now carries a **residual
clause: what it does not name is resolved toward review** — the work counts as unruled, belongs
to a new batch, and waits at three; the coordinator records the case. An unlisted state is then
a gap in the examples, not an instruction to do something unsafe. That changes what a reviewer
can fairly call blocking: a contradiction, a wrong row, an unsafe or unobeyable residual — not
the mere existence of a state no row names. The next brief says so, and says why.

**The convergence criterion, stated before the next review rather than after it.** If the next
review fails on a contradiction, a wrong row or the residual clause itself, that is repaired and
reviewed again. If its only findings are states the residual clause already resolves safely,
they are minors by the rule's own terms: they become rows, and the candidate is published.
