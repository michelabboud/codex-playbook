## Verdict

The restatement fixes the two states that failed the third review and makes the ordinary zero-to-three sequence far easier to follow. It still does **not** satisfy the requested law: a literal agent can admit a fourth unruled range after merging work from foreign open batches, can land post-review fixes without any review of those fix commits, and is told by normative row 12 that a gate-time docs fix may land outside a batch. Conversely, an authorized one-task hotfix or docs change with no plan allocation cannot be admitted at all.

The rule is therefore not publishable as law. Green tests establish the shell and textual contracts, not these state-machine properties.

## Findings

### 1. Both — Blocking — foreign open batches make the stated admission equivalence false

**Locations:** A `.agents/skills/codex-playbook-reviews/SKILL.md:45-46`, worked cases 5–7 at lines 56–58; B `rules/REVIEWS.md:51-52`, worked cases 5–7 at lines 62–64.

**Origin:** cold read.

The invariant counts every unruled batch with any commit reachable from the line. The state model calls a batch open from admission until closure, says one batch is open per line, and lets a branch cut from open A carry A while opening B on the branch. It does not define B's state after that branch merges back while B remains open on its owning line.

A concrete allowed walk breaks the claimed equivalence:

1. X opens A.
2. Y and Z branch from A; Y opens B and Z opens C. Each branch carries two identities and obeys the ceiling.
3. Y and Z merge into X while A is still open. The merge commits and conflict resolutions belong to A. X now carries `{A, B, C}` by ancestry and still obeys the three-identity invariant.
4. A closes. X has no locally open batch, but B and C remain open on their owning lines.
5. The sentence said to be equivalent — “a new batch starts only while at most two **closed** batches are unruled” — sees only closed A and permits D. X then carries four unruled identities.

Reading B and C as closed snapshots on X would prevent D, but the rule does not say that and pins no review targets for those supposed snapshots. Reading their actual source-line states makes X carry multiple open batches. Either reading defeats a literal, complete state machine.

**Do instead:** remove the “same as” closed-batch formulation and make admission test the invariant directly: no destination-owned open batch and at most two carried unruled identities. Then either:

- forbid merging commits from a source batch that is still open; or
- explicitly define an imported open batch as a frozen, unruled snapshot on the destination, identify the review that covers that snapshot, and say that it counts but does not consume the destination's one open slot.

The first option is simpler and safer. A merge into an already-open **destination** batch is otherwise sound: its merge commit and conflict resolution enter that batch, and the destination's later pinned target includes them.

### 2. Both — Blocking — attached fix commits have no mandatory review before ruling

**Locations:** A `.agents/skills/codex-playbook-reviews/SKILL.md:45`, worked cases 9 and 16 at lines 60 and 67; B `rules/REVIEWS.md:51`, worked cases 9 and 16 at lines 66 and 73. Post-read corroboration: A `docs/plans/2026-09-20-non-blocking-review-pipeline-port-plan.md:176`.

**Origin:** cold read; the plan corroboration is post-read.

Closure pins the review target and freezes membership. The exception then lets a finding-fix commit land “attached” to that closed batch. Ruling requires the blocker to be fixed or dismissed, but no canonical sentence makes the fix commit itself an input to a mechanical review, a focused deep re-review, or a renewed pinned candidate. Rechecking the original finding against current tip is not review of the fix's other effects.

The approved implementation plan explicitly says “Fixes are reviewed against the updated candidate,” but that obligation did not reach either canonical rule. A literal coordinator may accept a defective fix, observe that the original symptom is gone, and rule the batch. That lands unreviewable work by the rule's own pinned-target mechanics.

**Do instead:** keep the original batch target immutable; record each attached fix commit in the ledger; require a pinned, focused independent review of the cumulative original-target-plus-fixes candidate before ruling; and say who validates that review and how any new finding is disposed.

### 3. Both — Blocking — normative row 12 authorizes work outside a batch during a gate

**Locations:** A `.agents/skills/codex-playbook-reviews/SKILL.md:63`; B `rules/REVIEWS.md:69`.

**Origin:** cold read.

The table wins over prose. Row 12 says a ready docs fix “lands on a line not merged into the candidate” but never admits an open batch or attaches the change to an unruled one. A milestone drains the candidate line's lower count to zero before the gate, so there may be no unruled batch to receive the fix. The row therefore contradicts “nothing lands on a line outside a batch” and creates the exact unreviewable landing the invariant claims to forbid.

**Do instead:** say that the separate line must first admit a batch, that the docs fix lands in that batch and owes its normal reviews, and that merging it after the gate restarts the candidate checks and applicable review.

### 4. Both — Blocking — authorized work without a plan-allocated batch is unlandable

**Locations:** A `.agents/skills/codex-playbook-reviews/SKILL.md:45`; B `rules/REVIEWS.md:51`.

**Origin:** cold read.

An open batch is admitted only at first dispatch of “a task the plan allocates to it.” A first planned batch on a fresh line works. A routine one-task hotfix, docs correction, or other authorized single-task change for which the authority rules require no plan has no batch it can open. “Nothing lands outside a batch” then makes the work impossible. Requiring a new approved plan would contradict the plan exemption for ordinary single-task work and turn a review ceiling into a new approval process.

**Do instead:** allow the sole coordinator to admit an explicitly identified ad-hoc one-task batch when no approved plan is required. Put it in the same ledger and give it the same task/batch review obligations; no new approval gate is needed.

### 5. Both — Minor — current measurement and carrier text retains the old “ceiling of two” model

**Locations:** A `.agents/skills/codex-playbook-reviews/SKILL.md:79`; B `rules/REVIEWS.md:85`, `PROGRESS.md:17-20`, `docs/index.html:408`.

**Origin:** cold read.

The closing measurement sentence in both canonical review files still calls this “the ceiling of two.” B's current progress record says “the ceiling of two unreviewed batches,” and its visual ladder says “two closed batches at most, plus the one being built.” The invariant's ceiling is three unruled identities per line. Two is the ordinary number before admitting the third or the intended number in review while one is open; it is not the state invariant, and three closed/unruled batches is explicitly allowed.

**Do instead:** measure “how often a line carried three unruled identities” and, where useful, separately measure “how often admission occurred with two already carried.” Update B's progress and visual ladder to those terms.

### 6. Both — Minor — the explanatory guides still say the ceiling counts closed batches

**Locations:** A `docs/guides/non-blocking-review-pipeline.md:109-128,276-292`; B `docs/guides/non-blocking-review-pipeline.md:104-123,271-280`. Related A plan text: `docs/plans/2026-09-20-non-blocking-review-pipeline-port-plan.md:76-81`.

**Origin:** post-read.

Both guides later insert the new invariant, but their live rationale still says “the ceiling counts closed batches awaiting a ruling” and assesses whether “a ceiling of two” is right. That is the seam the restatement was expressly meant to remove. A's still-running detailed plan likewise describes rule 3.5 as a ceiling of two unruled batches under the tip. The historical corrections may remain, but the current explanation must not continue to present the superseded state model as the operative one.

**Do instead:** rewrite the live rationale around three admitted/unruled identities, explaining that two reviewed-or-reviewing ranges plus one destination-owned open range is the expected maximum shape, while three closed ranges is also valid. Mark older closed-batch wording explicitly historical or remove it from the operative explanation. Update the running plan's current-decision section without rewriting its historical evidence.

## Intricacy

The restatement is simpler than the prior version, but it is not yet obeyable as written. The remaining complexity comes from retaining two accounting models — total admitted/unruled identities and closed batches — and from treating fixes and gate-side work as exceptions without completing their review obligations.

The smallest durable rule would keep only these concepts:

1. A line carries a set of at most three unruled batch identities.
2. At most one batch is destination-owned and open; a new one admits only when none is open and the carried set has at most two identities.
3. Every landing belongs to that open batch, except recorded finding-fixes, which require their own pinned focused review before the attached batch can be ruled.
4. A merge from an open source batch is forbidden; every permitted merge belongs to the destination's open batch and its union must remain at most three.
5. Planned work uses its allocated batch; authorized unplanned single-task work uses a ledgered ad-hoc batch.

That cuts the false closed-batch equivalence, the imported-open ambiguity, and the implicit exceptions. It preserves the owner's exposure choice.
