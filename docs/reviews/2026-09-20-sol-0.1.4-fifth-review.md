## The four repairs

### Ad-hoc batches

The repair cannot textually launder planned work past the plan's batch boundaries. Planned work opens the batch allocated by the plan; the ad-hoc route is limited to already-authorized work that needs no plan. With an open batch, the unplanned task joins it and receives that batch's review; with no open batch and zero through two carried identities, the coordinator admits a named ad-hoc batch; at three it waits unless it is a recorded finding's fix. Row 18 covers those counts correctly and adds no approval gate.

### Reviewed fixes

A batch with fixes cannot be ruled merely because the original symptom disappeared. The original target stays pinned, every fix commit is recorded, and the cumulative pinned-target-plus-fixes candidate receives a focused review at the depth that found the defect. “Validated like any other” routes validation to the coordinator under rules 3.1 and 3.3.

The recursion is not capped, correctly: a focused review that finds a real defect records another finding, whose necessary fix remains attached to the same unruled batch and is reviewed again. At a count of three this work is expressly allowed, so the line has a permitted path to reduce the count. Repeated real findings can delay a ruling but do not create a policy deadlock.

### Lines and task worktrees

The distinction is decidable from dispatch provenance and the ledger. A task's worktree returns its result to the line and open batch from which it was dispatched. A separately admitted line inherits the carried set at its cut and opens its own batch. Only a source whose batch is closed may merge, the destination's reachable set is the union, a union over three waits, and the merge landing belongs to the destination's open batch. The task-worktree exception therefore does not reopen fan-out-and-merge-back around the ceiling.

### Gate-side work and row 12

Row 12 now agrees with both controlling rules. A gate-time docs fix lands on a non-candidate line only after a batch is admitted there, owes ordinary reviews, and remains outside the frozen candidate. Merging it changes the candidate and therefore restarts candidate checks and review.

## Residual clause and the twenty-row table

The residual clause is safe on the reviewed state space:

- It is conservative: unnamed work is unruled, belongs to a new batch, and cannot enter a line already carrying three.
- It does not override named prose or a worked row. The “row wins” rule resolves a conflict between specific prose and a specific row; row 20 applies only when neither a sentence nor another row supplies a transition.
- It does not consume the escape needed at three. A recorded finding's necessary fix and its focused review are specifically named exceptions, so they can rule a batch and reduce the count.
- It does not create approval. The new-batch fallback is review bookkeeping; authority still comes from the authority and planning rules.

No unsafe, contradictory, or deadlocking residual state was found. No additional candidate row was needed from this review.

All twenty rows agree with the prose. Rows 1–6, 8, 10, and 11 remain accurate despite retaining language from the earlier closed-batch presentation: their stated states happen to be closed, but none redefines the counted set. The deleted sentence, `a new batch starts only while at most two closed batches are unruled`, is absent from both operative rule files. Historical guides and decision records retain old wording only inside passages explicitly marked as history or superseded.

## Obeyability

A coordinator can execute the complete sequence from the rule text:

1. **Admit:** only with no open batch and at most two carried unruled identities; use the plan's batch or a ledgered ad-hoc batch.
2. **Land and close:** every ordinary landing goes to the open batch; closure freezes membership, pins the target, and dispatches deep review without changing the count.
3. **Rule:** validate every finding, resolve every blocker, and, if fixes landed, validate the required focused review before lowering the count.
4. **Merge:** accept only closed source work, keep the union at three or less, and put the merge landing in the destination's open batch.
5. **Fix at the ceiling:** attach only the changes necessary for a recorded finding to the batch being repaired, then focused-review them.
6. **Gate:** settle every lower review, drain the candidate line to zero, freeze it, and restart its checks and review after any later merge.

I would not cut any of those controls. The residual clause is the part that lets the rule stop growing a new normative row for every harmless combination while retaining a fail-safe answer.
