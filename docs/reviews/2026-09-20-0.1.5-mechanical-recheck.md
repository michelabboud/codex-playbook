### 3. Informational — `awk -v file=…` interprets backslash escapes

`tests/rulebook_test.sh:392`. A path containing a backslash would be altered in the message. `$reviews_file` is the constant `.agents/skills/codex-playbook-reviews/SKILL.md`; no effect. Noted only because you asked about backslashes: the heredocs are quoted (`<<'EOF'`), so `**`, the apostrophe in `line's open batch` and backslashes reach `read -r` and `grep -F` literally, and `IFS= read -r` keeps leading/trailing whitespace. A colon in a matched line does not disturb the location: `awk -F:` reads `$1` of `grep -n`'s `N:text` output, which is always the line number (M5 plants a sentence on `SKILL.md:45`, whose text contains a colon, and reports `:45`).
