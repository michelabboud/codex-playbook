## Findings so far

### Blocking — the ceiling admits a third closed, unruled batch

`.agents/skills/codex-playbook-reviews/SKILL.md:45` says both that a line may
carry at most two closed, unruled batches and that batch N+2 may build after N
and N+1 are closed, then says "when N+2 closes with two still unruled" the line
waits. At that instant N, N+1, and N+2 are all closed and unruled, so the rule
has already exceeded its own ceiling. The guide repeats the same transition at
`docs/guides/non-blocking-review-pipeline.md:122-135`.

I would say explicitly that with two closed/unruled batches, the third batch may
be built but cannot itself close (which the rule defines as review dispatched)
until one older batch is ruled; no fourth batch starts. If the intended policy
instead allows three closed batches, the stated ceiling and worst-case account
must change.

The assertion at `tests/rulebook_test.sh:295-301` still passes because it checks
only for the words `union`, `plus the one being built`, and `mechanical included`.
It does not exercise this state transition. Thus the test passes while the
accounting contract it claims to guard is internally broken.

### Blocking — the claimed single owner does not own the tier definitions

The roster and both consuming skills say the roster is the single owner and that
the skills do not restate its definitions
(`.agents/skills/codex-playbook-subagents/references/roster.md:3-7`,
`.agents/skills/codex-playbook-subagents/SKILL.md:12`). But the subagents skill
immediately defines the work trusted to Fast, Standard, Strong, and Top at
`.agents/skills/codex-playbook-subagents/SKILL.md:14-16`, duplicating the roster's
`Trusted with` column at `references/roster.md:14-19`. The reviews skill likewise
owns review-to-tier assignments at
`.agents/skills/codex-playbook-reviews/SKILL.md:18-27`.

I would choose one precise ownership boundary and enforce it. If the roster owns
responsibilities and assignments, the skills should refer to named roster rows
without repeating them. If numbered rules must own normative assignments, the
roster should own only dated runtime bindings and descriptive tier selection
criteria, and the "single owner" claim should be narrowed accordingly.

The test at `tests/rulebook_test.sh:277-286` finds only literal Markdown table
rows. It passes while equivalent prose definitions remain in `SKILL.md`, so its
"one owner" success message overstates the contract it verifies.

### Blocking — rule 3.1 and the stop-the-line rule can be obeyed opposite ways

`.agents/skills/codex-playbook-reviews/SKILL.md:25` says a mechanical review
"never blocks the next task". Lines 37 and 43 say a blocking finding stops the
line regardless of which kind of review found it. The table's "while it runs"
context suggests the intended distinction is pending review versus a returned
blocker, but the normative sentence does not say that. A literal-minded agent
can continue after a mechanical blocker while citing line 25.

I would replace it with: a pending mechanical review never delays the next task;
once any review returns a blocker, the stop-the-line rule applies regardless of
review kind.

### Blocking — the scratch-directory ancestry condition is not generally executable

`.agents/skills/codex-playbook-reviews/SKILL.md:39` requires a reviewer scratch
directory with "no other reviewer and no programme material anywhere above it".
Every ordinary directory has parents up to the filesystem root, and a Codex lane
normally cannot prove or control the absence of programme material across that
entire ancestry. The rule therefore demands a filesystem property that the
document's own sandbox discussion says cannot be assumed.

I would scope the condition to the reviewer-visible or task-managed scratch
subtree, require a dedicated parent with enumerated contents when that is
available, and require the review header to record when stronger isolation was
not enforceable.

### Minor — “no product names” is broader than the roster actually follows

The roster says "No product names live here" at
`.agents/skills/codex-playbook-subagents/references/roster.md:5`, but names Codex
at lines 12 and 61 and Claude at line 55. The measurement needs provenance, so
the likely contract is "no model identifiers in tier definitions or bindings",
not literally no product names. The README's "capabilities, never products"
claim at `README.md:44-46` should be narrowed the same way.
